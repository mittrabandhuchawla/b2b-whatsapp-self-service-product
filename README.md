
# Conversational Commerce: B2B Product Management Case Study

## Project Overview

This case study explores the product evolution of a WhatsApp-based customer self-service assistant for a B2B electrical distribution business.

The existing product foundation focused on simplifying customer support and commercial information access through structured conversational workflows.

The proposed next iteration expands the experience toward AI-assisted product discovery, natural-language order interpretation, and conversational commerce.

**Domain:** B2B Distribution and Commerce  
**Role:** Product Requirements, Solution Design and Implementation Coordination  
**Focus:** Product Management, Digital Transformation and Business Process Automation  
**Project Type:** Real-world product initiative, documented through a public, anonymized case study

> Note: This repository contains a generalized product case study based on professional experience. It is not the production source code. The next-generation capabilities described are proposed requirements, not claims of completed deployment.

---

## 1. Business Problem

Traditional B2B distribution often depends on manual customer interactions for routine transactions.

Customers may need to contact sales or support teams to:

- Request product information or price lists
- Retrieve commercial documents
- Submit inquiries or quotations
- Find the relevant sales representative
- Raise service complaints
- Check account-related information

This creates repetitive work, additional customer effort and operational dependencies on internal teams.

The product opportunity was to make WhatsApp a more useful self-service channel while preserving the business controls required for commercial transactions.

## 2. Existing Product Foundation

The initial product included documented capabilities and workflows covering:

- Customer identification and account selection
- Outstanding statement requests
- Credit and debit note access
- Invoice-related requests
- Price-list and brochure distribution
- Request-for-quotation capture
- Complaint registration
- Branch and account-manager information
- New-customer inquiry capture
- English and Hindi menu-based interactions

This foundation informed the proposed product evolution.

The scope of this case study does not independently establish the live deployment status of every documented capability.

## 3. Product Vision

Create a conversational digital entry point where customers can discover products, request business information and initiate transactions without needing to understand the organization's internal systems.

The future experience should support:

1. Natural-language product discovery
2. Product identification without exact SKU knowledge
3. Hindi, English and Hinglish interactions
4. Structured order interpretation
5. Basket review and confirmation
6. Approved account-specific commercial information
7. New-business inquiry and onboarding requests
8. Escalation to human teams when needed

The vision is to reduce unnecessary manual interactions while improving convenience, accuracy and customer control.

## 4. Product Evolution

| Dimension | Existing Foundation | Proposed Evolution |
|---|---|---|
| Interaction | Structured menus | Natural-language assistance with menu fallback |
| Product discovery | Primarily support and information journeys | AI-assisted product discovery |
| Orders | Inquiry and RFQ capture | Conversational basket and order submission |
| Languages | English and Hindi menus | English, Hindi and Hinglish understanding |
| Customer identification | Account lookup | Context-aware customer classification |
| Service | Document requests and complaint capture | Simplified conversational self-service |
| Exceptions | Existing workflow handling | Clarification, safeguards and human escalation |

The second column describes the documented existing foundation. The third describes the proposed product requirements, not delivered outcomes.

## 5. Product Management Responsibilities

Work associated with this initiative included:

- Translating commercial and operational needs into product requirements
- Defining target customer journeys and use cases
- Structuring business rules, exceptions and system dependencies
- Evaluating how existing workflows could support future capabilities
- Coordinating requirements across business and technology stakeholders
- Contributing to product evolution and implementation planning

This case study focuses on product and business ownership rather than claiming authorship of production software.

## 6. Key Product Decisions

### Preserve useful existing functionality

Avoid rebuilding working customer-service journeys when they can be reused or improved.

### Keep AI separate from commercial authority

AI may help interpret customer requests, but final pricing, customer eligibility and transaction decisions must come from approved business systems.

### Require explicit customer confirmation

A customer must review and confirm an order before the system submits it.

### Handle uncertainty transparently

Ambiguous product requests should trigger clarification or human assistance instead of unsupported assumptions.

### Protect customer information

Customer-specific information must only be shared following approved identification and authorization checks.

### Design for operational handoffs

New inquiries, onboarding requests and exceptions must reach the appropriate business teams.

## 10. Key Takeaway

The primary product challenge was not simply introducing AI into a messaging interface.

It was designing a reliable customer journey across product discovery, account identification, commercial rules, transaction confirmation and operational handoffs.

This case illustrates how product management connects customer experience with the realities of enterprise operations.
