# Product Launch, Go-to-Market & Sales Enablement

## Overview

Launching a digital self-service product in a traditional B2B distribution environment requires more than building the technology.

Customers need to understand why they should use the product, while internal teams need clarity on its functionality, limitations, and role within existing sales and service processes.

This case study documents the go-to-market and enablement approach for a WhatsApp-based customer self-service assistant.

## My Role

I owned the initiative end-to-end, including product ideation, requirements, implementation coordination, launch, promotion, and adoption-building activities.

My responsibilities spanned both product delivery and the business-side work required to introduce the solution to customers and internal teams.

## 1. Customer-Facing Promotion

A customer communication asset was created to introduce the assistant and explain its benefits.

The communication emphasized:

- 24/7 self-service availability
- Quick access to routine commercial information
- Reduced dependence on manual follow-ups for supported requests
- A familiar WhatsApp-based interface
- Simple instructions for getting started

The material included a feature overview and a three-step onboarding journey:

1. Open the WhatsApp conversation.
2. Send a greeting to initiate the assistant.
3. Select the required service from the menu.

**Product Management Significance:** The communication translated technology capabilities into tangible customer benefits and reduced the effort required to understand and start using the product.

## 2. Internal Sales Enablement

A structured 13-slide sales briefing was prepared to align frontline teams on the product's purpose and operating model.

The briefing covered:

- Available self-service features
- Customer identification and account selection
- Differences between automated and human-assisted interactions
- Supported commercial-document requests
- Product limitations and exception handling
- When to direct customers to the assistant
- When to retain account-manager involvement
- Consistent customer-facing communication

**Product Management Significance:** Adoption depended on ensuring that sales teams understood how the product complemented, rather than replaced, their responsibilities.

## 3. Customer Segmentation

The experience distinguished between existing customers and new business inquiries.

Existing customers could be identified through their registered contact details and associated account context.

Where a customer was associated with multiple accounts, an account-selection step was used before accessing account-specific services.

New prospects followed a separate information-capture and general self-service journey.

This design addressed a practical B2B complexity: one contact may represent multiple business relationships.

## 4. Automation vs. Human Support

A key operating principle was to automate well-defined routine tasks while preserving human ownership of commercially sensitive or ambiguous requests.

**Automated service examples:**

- Price-list access
- Outstanding statement retrieval
- Invoice and credit/debit note copies
- Branch information
- General company information

**Human-assisted examples:**

- Special pricing discussions
- Complex product inquiries
- Exceptions outside supported document access
- Urgent or relationship-sensitive matters

This distinction was incorporated into internal enablement material to help teams set accurate customer expectations.

## 5. Managing Product Limitations

Customer adoption requires trust. Overpromising product capabilities can create additional support work and undermine confidence.

The sales briefing explicitly clarified that the assistant did not provide unlimited historical ledger access or independently negotiate customer-specific pricing.

The enablement approach therefore emphasized communicating what the product could reliably do, alongside when human assistance remained appropriate.

## 6. Adoption Strategy

The documented adoption approach combined:

**Customer education:** Explain the benefits and provide a straightforward onboarding path.

**Sales advocacy:** Give frontline representatives consistent guidance on when to recommend self-service.

**Expectation management:** Prevent customers from using automated channels for requests outside their supported scope.

**Operational alignment:** Maintain clear responsibility for requests requiring human intervention.

**Continuous improvement:** Use customer interactions, observed issues, and product usage to inform further refinement.
